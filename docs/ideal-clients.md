# CALIX — Ideal Client Profiles

**Status:** Working hypotheses, pending portfolio validation (see `docs/portfolio-content-inventory.md`).
**Last updated:** 2026-10-09
**Market:** International, English-speaking.
**Objective:** Qualified direct project enquiries.

> These profiles are built from the confirmed brief (positioning, services, premium visual direction, enquiry objective). They are **not** derived from the client portfolio, because none has been supplied. Every profile is defined by the client's **situation**, not by an invented industry. Industries are added only when the portfolio supports them (§5).

## 1. Primary ICP — the "outgrown" established business

**Who:** An established business whose offer has matured, but whose brand and website still reflect an earlier stage. They sell something considered and higher-value (a service, product or experience where the buyer researches before contacting), and they depend on their website to generate enquiries or sales.

| Attribute | Profile |
|---|---|
| Stage | Operating and trading, typically several years in. Past the "first logo and template site" stage. |
| Decision-maker | Founder, managing director or head of marketing. Usually one or two people decide. |
| Buying style | Considered purchase. They compare two to four studios, look closely at portfolio and process, and want to talk to the people doing the work. |
| Budget | `[TO CONFIRM: minimum project size CALIX will accept]`. Must be able to fund strategy, identity and a website as one engagement. |
| Geography | International, English-speaking. `[TO CONFIRM: time zones and regions CALIX can serve well]` |
| Internal team | Small or no in-house design or development team. May have one marketing generalist. |

**Why primary:**
- It's the situation where all five services connect: the brand needs repositioning, the website must express it, search must find it, and conversion must turn traffic into enquiries.
- The premium editorial visual direction suits buyers who care about quality signals.
- Project budgets are larger than single-service work.
- It leads naturally into post-launch SEO/GEO and growth work.

### Pain points
- "Our work is better than our website suggests." The brand undersells the business, and they lose pitches or prices to competitors who look more credible.
- The website generates few or poor-quality enquiries. It gets traffic but no conversations, or conversations with the wrong clients.
- Brand, website, ads and SEO have been bought from different suppliers over time. Nothing is consistent, and nobody owns the whole picture.
- The site is hard to update, slow, inaccessible or technically outdated.
- They have heard about AI search (ChatGPT, Perplexity, AI Overviews) and don't know whether they are visible there or what to do about it.
- They have been burned before: a beautiful site that didn't perform, or an SEO agency that promised rankings.

### Purchase triggers
- Repositioning: moving upmarket, launching a new service line, entering a new market or country.
- A growth event: investment, a new leadership hire, a merger or rebrand.
- A visible failure: a lost pitch, declining enquiries, a competitor relaunch, or a site that breaks.
- A platform deadline: end of support for a CMS or theme, a security problem, an e-commerce migration.
- A new marketing lead who wants to fix the foundations before spending on acquisition.

### Objections to answer on the site
| Objection | What answers it |
|---|---|
| "Have you done this for a business like mine?" | Relevant P1/P2 case studies. Industry or situation tags on the work page. |
| "Will it actually bring enquiries, or just look nice?" | Conversion-led process and measured outcomes (E3) where they exist. Never guarantees. |
| "What will it cost?" | Published starting ranges or a minimum engagement. `[TO CONFIRM]` |
| "Who will I actually work with?" | Named team with real roles. |
| "What happens if I enquire?" | A clear description of the next step, plus a reply time. `[TO CONFIRM]` |

## 2. Secondary ICP A — the launch

**Who:** A new venture, new product line or spin-out brand that needs identity, website and launch visibility from zero. This includes founders with funding or a committed budget, and established companies launching a sub-brand.

| Attribute | Profile |
|---|---|
| Decision-maker | Founder, co-founders, or a brand or marketing lead inside a parent company. |
| Need | Brand Strategy & Identity plus Web Design & Development, with launch SEO foundations. Possibly launch campaigns (Digital Marketing). |
| Risk | Budgets vary a lot. Early-stage founders may want premium output at template prices. |

**Pain points:** No identity yet, a launch date approaching, unsure how to look credible from day one, and no in-house team.
**Triggers:** Funding round, product readiness, launch date set, a market entry.
**Why secondary:** It's a strong fit for brand-plus-web work, but there's no existing traffic or baseline, so measurable outcomes take longer to prove. There's also a higher risk of low-budget enquiries. Qualify on budget early.

## 3. Secondary ICP B — the underperforming good brand

**Who:** A business with a credible brand and a reasonable website, which is not converting or not being found. They need SEO & GEO and/or Growth & Conversion, not a rebuild.

| Attribute | Profile |
|---|---|
| Decision-maker | Head of marketing, e-commerce manager, or founder. |
| Need | Technical and content SEO, GEO practices, analytics, conversion optimisation, landing pages. Possibly paid campaigns. |
| Engagement shape | Audit, then a prioritised programme, often as a retainer. |

**Pain points:** Traffic without enquiries; declining organic visibility; no reliable analytics; no idea whether AI search mentions them.
**Triggers:** A drop in leads or sales, an algorithm change, a new marketing budget, a board asking for measurable digital performance.
**Why secondary:** It offers recurring revenue and is a good follow-on for primary ICP clients. But as a **first** contact it puts CALIX in direct competition with specialist SEO and performance agencies that have deeper track records, unless CALIX's portfolio holds measured search or conversion results (E3).

## 4. Who CALIX is not for (disqualifiers)

Stating these publicly and in the enquiry form reduces unqualified enquiries.

- Buyers seeking **only** a logo or a single banner at a commodity price.
- Buyers asking for **guaranteed** rankings, guaranteed AI mentions or guaranteed ad returns.
- Projects below the minimum engagement `[TO CONFIRM]`.
- Requests that need capabilities CALIX does not have in-house or through a verified partner (see `docs/strategy.md` §9). Examples might include native mobile apps, complex enterprise systems integration or large-scale media buying, `[TO CONFIRM]`.
- Work that conflicts with CALIX's ethics or reputation `[TO DEFINE]`.

## 5. Priority industries

**Status: cannot be determined yet.** The brief requires industries to be based on the actual portfolio, and no portfolio data has been supplied.

**Method once the inventory is complete** (from `docs/portfolio-content-inventory.md` §7):

| Tier | Rule | Use on the site |
|---|---|---|
| Priority | Two or more P1/P2 projects in the industry, at least one at evidence grade E2 or better | Named in positioning and on service pages. May get a dedicated landing page later. |
| Emerging | One P1/P2 project | Shown in the work section, tagged by industry, not claimed as a specialism. |
| Untested | No projects | Not mentioned as an industry focus. |

**Hypotheses to test, not claims:** The premium editorial direction tends to suit industries where perceived quality drives the purchase and the website is a primary trust signal. Examples include professional and advisory services, design-led consumer and lifestyle brands, hospitality, property, and B2B firms selling high-value services. **None of these should be used in copy unless the portfolio supports them.**

**Guardrail:** If priority industries include hospitality, property or architecture-adjacent clients, copy must make clear that CALIX is a digital branding and growth studio that *serves* those sectors. It must not read as working in them (`calix-geo` classification rule).

## 6. Qualification criteria (what makes an enquiry "qualified")

Proposed definition, `[TO CONFIRM]`:

| Criterion | Qualified if |
|---|---|
| Need | Matches at least one of the five services, ideally the primary entry service. |
| Budget | At or above the minimum engagement for that service. |
| Timeline | A start date within `[X]` months. |
| Authority | The enquirer is the decision-maker or directly involved in the decision. |
| Fit | Not on the disqualifier list (§4). |

These criteria drive the enquiry-form fields in `docs/strategy.md` §8 and the internal lead-scoring step.
