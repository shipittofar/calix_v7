## 2. Publication levels

Every project must be assigned exactly one level before it appears anywhere on the site.

| Level | Meaning | Allowed on the site |
|---|---|---|
| **P1 — Public, named** | Client approved naming and publishing the work. | Client name, logo, imagery, approved facts and approved metrics. |
| **P2 — Public, anonymised** | Work may be shown, but the client must not be identified. | Industry descriptor (e.g. "a boutique hotel group"), imagery with identifying marks removed, facts with no identifying detail. |
| **P3 — Private / NDA** | Must not be published. | Nothing on the public site. May be mentioned in private sales conversations if the contract allows. |
| **C — Concept** | Self-initiated or unrealised work, not client-commissioned. | May be shown **only** with an explicit "Concept" label. Never presented as client work or given results. |

## 3. Evidence grades

| Grade | What it means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **E3 — Measured** | Outcome with a number, a source, a time period and a baseline. | "Organic enquiries rose from [X] to [Y] per month, [period], source: [GA4 / CRM]." |
| **E2 — Documented** | Deliverables and process are shown, with no measured outcome. | Identity system, site screenshots, process artefacts. |
| **E1 — Described** | Only a description of what was done. | "We redesigned the website." |
| **Q — Client statement** | A real quote, used with written permission and attributed as approved. | Never paraphrased into something the client did not say. |

Metrics are only published at E3, with client approval. A metric without a baseline, period or source is not published.

## 5. Questions for each project

Answer these for every project you want considered. "Unknown" and "cannot share" are both valid answers.

**Identity and permission**
1. What is the client name, and can it be published (P1), anonymised (P2), or not used publicly (P3)?
2. Is written permission on file? If not, who at the client can grant it?
3. Are there NDA or contract restrictions on showing the work, naming the client, or quoting figures?
4. Was this client-commissioned work, or a concept (C)?

**Context**
5. What industry is the client in, and what is their market or geography?
6. Roughly what size is the client (for internal segmentation only, never published without approval)?
7. Who was the decision-maker (role, not name), and how did the client find CALIX (referral, search, outreach, repeat client)?
8. What problem or trigger prompted the project?

**Scope and role**
9. Which of the five services did CALIX actually deliver?
10. Was CALIX the lead agency, a sub-contractor, or a partner to another agency? Which parts were done by third parties?
11. Who on the team did the work, and in what role?
12. When did the project start and end, and is any work ongoing (a retainer)?
13. What was the approximate fee band? This is for internal pricing calibration only, never published.

**Outcomes**
14. Is there any measured outcome (enquiries, conversion rate, organic traffic, rankings, revenue, page speed, accessibility score)? For each one: the baseline, the result, the time period, the data source, and whether other factors (seasonality, a parallel ad campaign) could explain it.
15. May that metric be published?
16. Is there a client quote, and may it be published with attribution?

**Assets**
17. What assets exist: identity files, guidelines, photography (and who holds the rights), site screenshots, a live URL, process artefacts (workshops, sitemaps, wireframes)?
18. Is the live site still running CALIX's work, or has it since been changed by others?

## 6. Questions about the portfolio as a whole

1. How many client projects exist in total? How many can be at P1 or P2?
2. Which **one to three** projects best represent the work CALIX wants more of?
3. Which projects represent work CALIX does **not** want more of? These should not be featured, even if they are good.
4. Which services appear most often across the projects? Which are rare or absent?
5. Which industries recur? An industry with two or more P1/P2 projects is a candidate priority industry.
6. Which projects involved more than one service? These are the evidence for CALIX's integrated positioning.
7. Do any current or past clients have ongoing retainers? For which services?
8. What share of past work came from referrals versus inbound enquiries?

## 7. How this inventory feeds the strategy

| Strategy decision | Rule applied once the inventory is filled |
|---|---|
| Priority industries | An industry is a **priority** with two or more P1/P2 projects, and at least one of them at E2 or better. It is **emerging** with one such project. Industries with no projects are not marketed as specialisms. |
| Primary entry service | Confirmed when it appears in the most P1/P2 projects **and** CALIX wants more of that work. If the evidence points elsewhere, revisit `docs/strategy.md` §5. |
| Integrated positioning claim | Credible only if at least two P1/P2 projects show CALIX delivering three or more of the five services. |
| Results claims | Only E3 metrics with publication approval. Everything else is described as scope and approach, not outcome. |
| Services shown as core | A service with no supporting project is presented as supporting or emerging, not headline. |
