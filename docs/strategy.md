## 4. Differentiation

### 4.1 The competitive frame

Prospects usually compare CALIX against three kinds of supplier. Each solves one part of the problem and leaves a gap at the hand-off.

| Competitor type | What they do well | Where it breaks for the client | CALIX difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Branding-only studio** | Positioning, identity, guidelines | Stops at the guidelines PDF. The website is built by someone else, who dilutes the identity. Nobody measures whether the brand brings enquiries. | Brand decisions are carried into the website, content structure and search presence by the same team, and judged by enquiries as well as appearance. |
| **Web development agency** | Builds, platforms, technical delivery | Builds to the client's brief, without challenging positioning or messaging. The launch is the finish line. | The website starts from strategy and messaging, and it's built with search, accessibility, performance and conversion as requirements, not add-ons. |
| **Performance-marketing agency** | Paid acquisition, reporting, campaign optimisation | Buys traffic toward a weak brand and a low-converting site. Results depend on ongoing spend. | Fixes what the traffic lands on first, then grows visibility through organic search and conversion improvements that keep working. Paid media is used where it makes sense, not as the default. |

### 4.2 The differentiation claim

> **One strategy, carried all the way through.** CALIX doesn't hand off between brand, website and growth. The positioning decided in the first workshop shapes the identity, the pages, the search structure and the enquiry journey.

### 4.3 Conditions for this claim to be true

The claim must not run ahead of reality. It holds only if:

1. **The portfolio shows it:** at least two publishable projects where CALIX delivered three or more of the five services (`docs/portfolio-content-inventory.md` §7).
2. **The team can deliver it:** the capabilities in §9 are confirmed as in-house or delivered through named, accountable partners.
3. **The process exists:** a documented method that links the phases, published as CALIX's process (§7).

If condition 1 isn't met at launch, use the softer, still-true version: "Strategy, design, technology and growth, planned together." Present it as how CALIX works, not as a track record.

### 4.4 Secondary differentiators (to be verified)

- **Responsible AI search visibility.** GEO is offered with explicit honesty: no guaranteed AI mentions, a focus on entity clarity and evidence. This stands out in a field of overpromising. CALIX's own site should demonstrate the practice.
- **Quality as standard.** Accessibility (WCAG 2.2 AA), performance and security are delivered as baseline. This is provable on CALIX's own site, which is the one case study available from day one.
- **Senior attention.** `[TO CONFIRM: is the client working directly with senior people?]` Only claim this if it's true.

## 5. Service packaging and cross-service opportunities

### 5.1 Commercial importance is not equal

| Service | Role | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| **Brand Strategy & Identity** | **Core** (half of the entry offer) | It's the visual direction the brand is built on, and it's highly visible in the portfolio. It starts most other work. |
| **Web Design & Development** | **Core** (half of the entry offer) | The website is where brand, search and conversion meet, and it's the most common trigger for buying. Larger project values. |
| **SEO & GEO** | **Supporting, growing** | Foundations are included in every website project. As a standalone retainer it's the main recurring-revenue route. As a first-contact service it competes with specialists, which needs measured results. |
| **Growth & Conversion** | **Supporting** | The natural post-launch continuation, and what ties work to the enquiry objective. Usually sold to existing clients or via the Diagnostic. |
| **Digital Marketing** | **Supporting, capability-dependent** | Crowded market, spend-dependent results, and hard to prove without metrics. Offer it on the scale the team can verifiably deliver (§9). Don't headline it. |

Creditable against a follow-on project `[TO CONFIRM]`. |

### 5.3 Cross-service paths

```
Digital Diagnostic ──► Brand & Website ──► Search & Growth Programme
        │                                         ▲
        └──► Website  or  Brand Identity ─────────┘
                                │
                                └──► Campaign creatives / Digital Marketing
```

- **Every website project includes SEO/GEO foundations and conversion tracking.** This is both quality practice and the setup for a later growth retainer, since the data to show what to improve already exists.
- **Every project ends with a growth review** after launch (for example at 60–90 days `[TO CONFIRM]`), based on real analytics. It's the natural, non-pushy moment to propose a retainer.
- **Brand identity work extends into campaign creatives and banners.** This is real recurring production work that keeps the identity consistent.
- **The Diagnostic costs are credited** against a follow-on project `[TO CONFIRM]`, so it's a low-risk first step.

### 5.4 Confirming the entry service against the portfolio

The Brand & Website recommendation is a hypothesis based on the brief's visual direction, the enquiry objective and the competitive logic in §4. It is **not** based on portfolio evidence. After the inventory is complete:

| If the portfolio shows… | Then… |
|---|---|
| Most P1/P2 projects combine brand and web | Confirm Brand & Website as the primary entry. |
| Mostly websites on clients' existing brands | Make **Website** (with SEO/GEO foundations) the primary entry, and keep brand as an upsell. |
| Mostly identity work | Make **Brand Identity** the primary entry, and present web as the continuation. |
| Measured (E3) search or conversion results | Raise SEO & GEO and Growth to co-primary, with the Diagnostic as the lead offer. |
| Little multi-service work | Use the softer differentiation claim (§4.3) and lead with the strongest single service. |

## 8. Conversion strategy for high-intent prospects

### 8.1 Visitor intent tiers

| Tier | Visitor question | Primary action offered |
|---|---|---|
| **Ready** (referral, returning, comparing two or three studios) | "Can they do my project, and what happens next?" | **Start a project**: the brief form |
| **Evaluating** (on service and work pages) | "Have they done this for someone like me?" | **See the work**, then Start a project. Or **Book an introductory call**. |
| **Problem-aware, not ready** | "What's actually wrong with our brand or website?" | **Request a Digital Diagnostic** |

There is one primary CTA site-wide: "Start a project". Others are secondary and visually quieter (`conversion-strategist` §2, §6).

### 8.2 Enquiry form (draft fields)

Short enough to finish in about two minutes, with fields that qualify against `docs/ideal-clients.md` §6.

| Field | Required | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Name | Yes | Contact |
| Work email | Yes | Contact |
| Company and website URL | Yes | Lets CALIX review before replying |
| What do you need? (multi-select: the five services, plus "Not sure yet") | Yes | Routing |
| Tell us about the project | Yes | Context |
| Budget range (bands `[TO CONFIRM]`, currency `[TO CONFIRM]`) | Yes | Qualification |
| Ideal start date (bands) | Yes | Qualification |
| How did you hear about CALIX? | No | Attribution |

The submit label is "Send my project details", not "Submit". Next to the button: "We reply within `[X]` working days with next steps." `[TO CONFIRM response time]`

### 8.3 Page-level conversion rules

- **Service pages** answer, in order: who it's for, what's included, how it works, relevant work, starting price or minimum `[TO CONFIRM]`, CTA. Each one includes a "who this is not for" line.
- **Case studies** end with a CTA tied to that project ("Start a similar project").
- **The contact page** explains what happens after submitting (reply, call, proposal), with a timeline.
- No newsletter popups, chat widgets or social icons competing with the primary CTA on high-intent pages.
- Scroll storytelling never hides or delays a CTA (`scroll-storytelling` ground rule 5).

### 8.4 Lead handling (operational, outside the website)

- Respond within the promised time. Response speed matters a great deal for considered purchases.
- Score each enquiry against the qualification criteria. Unqualified enquiries get a polite, useful reply (for example a pointer to the Diagnostic or a referral).
- Track the path: source → enquiry → qualified → call → proposal → won. Record it in a CRM or simple tracker `[TO CONFIRM tool]`.

### 8.5 Success measures

| Measure | Use |
|---|---|
| **Qualified enquiries per month** | Primary KPI |
| Qualification rate (qualified ÷ all enquiries) | Checks whether pages and form filter well |
| Enquiry-to-proposal and proposal-to-won rates | Commercial health |
| Average project value of won work | Checks positioning is attracting the right size of client |
| Conversion rate on service, work and contact pages | Optimisation input |
| Organic visibility for service and situation queries; AI-search mentions (monitored, never promised) | Leading indicators only |

Traffic volume, social followers and impressions are **not** KPIs.

**Compliance:** Analytics and form handling must respect GDPR, UK GDPR and equivalent rules for an international audience: consent, a privacy notice, data minimisation. Specify this in the technical phase.

## 11. The five strategic decisions that most affect positioning and lead generation

1. **The primary entry offer.** Confirm Brand & Website as the lead service, or switch based on what the portfolio actually shows (§5.4). This sets the homepage message, navigation emphasis, case-study selection and the kind of enquiry CALIX attracts.

2. **Focus: by situation or by industry.** Decide whether CALIX positions on client situation for now (outgrown, launching, underperforming), or commits to one or two priority industries the portfolio can support. An industry focus converts better but narrows reach. It's only allowed where the work backs it.

3. **What counts as "in-house".** Confirm the capability split (§9), especially for backend development, e-commerce, paid media and social media management. This decides whether "connects strategy, design, technology and growth" is literally true. It also determines whether Digital Marketing is a headline service or a supporting one.

4. **Price transparency and minimum engagement.** Decide whether to publish starting prices or budget bands, and set the minimum project size. This is the strongest single lever on enquiry quality, and it shapes the enquiry form.

5. **Which client work can be published, and with what proof.** Get permissions, choose two to four flagship case studies (ideally multi-service), and confirm which outcomes can be stated with numbers. Without this, the credibility step of every page is empty, and the integrated differentiation claim must stay in its softer form.
